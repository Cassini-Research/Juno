# Writing Workflows

Use these patterns for common writing tasks.

---

## Capture a quick note

**Use case:** Capture a short idea without switching away from the current app.

**The workflow:**
1. Press Fn / Globe from the current app.
2. Speak the idea in full.
3. Juno inserts the text in the focused field, or shows Copy Ready if no safe field is available.
4. Continue working.

This keeps short notes and follow-ups close to the work that prompted them.

**Recommended setup:** Open Actions and enable Notes if you want **Hey Juno** captures to land directly in Apple Notes as a running inbox instead of wherever the cursor happens to be.

---

## Draft an email

**Use case:** Draft a reply or new message from spoken context.

**The workflow:**
1. Open Mail and start a new reply or compose
2. Click into the body
3. Press Fn / Globe and speak the message content: *"Marcus, following up on the API question from last week. We can support the webhook format, and the implementation looks straightforward. We will confirm timing once it is in the sprint."*
4. Press Fn/Globe to stop. Juno places a properly formatted email — punctuated, paragraphed, professionally toned.

Speak the substance in your normal voice. Formal email style translates it into professional prose — full sentences, removed filler, structured for readability.

A longer email can be drafted in one session or across multiple pauses.

---

## Take meeting notes

**Use case:** Capture decisions, action items, and important details during a meeting.

**The workflow:**
1. Have Apple Notes or Notion open in the background
2. When a decision, action item, or key detail is mentioned, press Fn / Globe, dictate it, then press Fn / Globe again
3. Use `next bullet` between items so the note builds structurally without keyboard contact
4. At the end: review, edit the few things that need it, and the notes are done

**Example stream:**

> *"Sarah approved the revised budget. Next bullet. James owns the vendor outreach, deadline end of this month. Next bullet. Deferred the roadmap conversation to Thursday call."*

Juno creates structured notes from the spoken items.

**Recommended setup:** Pin Structured notes style to Apple Notes.

---

## Journal by voice

**Use case:** Capture journal entries in a natural voice and let Juno remove common speech artifacts.

**The workflow:**
1. Open Day One or Apple Notes
2. Press Fn / Globe and speak the entry.
3. Let Juno remove filler, punctuation issues, and repeated phrases while preserving the substance.

Use the default style for natural prose, or a custom style if you want a specific journal format.

---

## Processing research and interviews

**Use case:** Summarize interview notes, articles, or research while reviewing source material.

**The workflow:**
1. Read the source material with your cursor in your synthesis document
2. Speak your analysis as you read: *"her main point seems to be that the bottleneck is not capacity, it is coordination — she keeps coming back to this idea that teams have the resources but not the visibility into how they connect, that feels important for the positioning argument"*
3. Juno places clean prose continuously as you speak
4. At the end, you have a first draft of the synthesis

**With selected text:** Highlight the key quote → press Fn/Globe → say *"translate this into a one-line insight"* → Juno rewrites the selection as your note.

---

## Turn voice memos into drafts

**Use case:** Convert existing spoken notes into a written draft.

**The workflow:**

1. Back at your desk, open the voice memo and your writing surface
2. Play the memo through your speaker or one earbud
3. Press Fn / Globe and re-speak the key ideas as you listen
4. Juno places clean prose as you speak; the memo fills in the gaps you forget

Use this when the goal is a clean draft rather than a verbatim transcript.

---

## Draft long-form writing

**Use case:** Draft several paragraphs before switching into editing mode.

**The workflow:**
1. Speak a full section, such as three to five paragraphs, in a single dictation session.
2. Use `new paragraph` to separate sections without pausing
3. Do not try to edit while dictating — keep going
4. Press Fn/Globe to finish and read the full result

Review and edit after the section is complete.

---

## Code comments and documentation

**Use case:** Add comments, docstrings, or documentation while reviewing code.

**The workflow:**
1. Select a function or block in your editor
2. Press Fn/Globe: *"explain that this checks whether the saved session is still active, renews it when needed, and returns early when renewal fails"*
3. Press Fn/Globe to finish

With a custom Code Comment style, Juno produces a clean inline comment in the right format for your codebase.

---

## Rephrasing on the fly

**Use case:** Rephrase selected text without switching tools.

**The workflow:**
1. Select the sentence
2. Press Fn/Globe: say what you mean differently, or say a command: *"make that more direct"* or *"remove the hedge"*
3. Juno replaces the selection with the rewrite

This is useful for single-sentence edits where the intended meaning is clear and only the framing needs to change.

---

## Standup updates

**Use case:** Draft a concise standup update before a meeting.

**The workflow:**
1. Open Slack or your standup tool
2. 30 seconds before joining: Fn/Globe → speak what you did yesterday, what you are doing today, any blockers
3. Use your Standup custom style with `next bullet` between sections
4. The update is ready in the compose field before the call starts

The update reflects your current work before the context switch into the meeting.

---

## Snippets for recurring text

**Use case:** Expand recurring text by voice, including scheduling links, addresses, sign-offs, and standard message templates.

**Recommended setup:** Use snippets.

In Settings → Dictionary and Memory → Snippets, create a trigger → body entry for each recurring phrase:

| Trigger | Body |
| --- | --- |
| `my calendar link` | your actual scheduling link |
| `our address` | your company's full mailing address |
| `project sign-off` | Thanks for the partnership on this — looking forward to the next step. |

When you say "my calendar link" in any dictation, Juno expands it to the full text.

**Snippet scopes:** Snippets can be global (expand everywhere) or scoped to messaging, email, docs, or code. A code-scoped snippet only expands when you are dictating in a code context, so you do not get code boilerplate appearing in your emails.

## Working in multiple languages

If you work in more than one language, start with **Auto-detect** in Settings → Writing & language. Juno identifies the language per session.

If you naturally mix English and Hindi in the same sentence, select the **Hindi + English** code-switching pair — both languages are recognized simultaneously, no setting changes mid-session.

The picker today offers five languages — Auto-detect, English, Spanish, Mandarin Chinese, and Hindi + English (code-switching pair) — plus a separate **Keep original** translation toggle. See [Settings](/docs/use-juno/settings).
